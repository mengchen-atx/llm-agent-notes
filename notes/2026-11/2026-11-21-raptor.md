# RAPTOR: retrieve from a tree of summaries, not just flat chunks

**Paper:** Sarthi, Abdullah, Tuli, Khanna, Goldie & Manning, "RAPTOR: Recursive Abstractive Processing for Tree-Organized Retrieval" ([arXiv:2401.18059](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.18059)) · code: [parthsarthi03/raptor](https://github.com/parthsarthi03/raptor)

## Idea
Standard RAG retrieves a few short contiguous chunks, which is fine for lookup questions but bad for anything that needs the gist of a whole document. RAPTOR builds a tree bottom-up: split the text into ~100-token chunks (never cutting a sentence), embed them with SBERT (multi-qa-mpnet-base-cos-v1), cluster the embeddings, have an LLM summarize each cluster, then embed and cluster the summaries again, until clustering stops making sense. Leaves hold details; upper nodes hold themes.

## Why it matters
Questions about a long story or paper often need information spread across many places. With GPT-4 as the reader, RAPTOR reaches 82.6% accuracy on QuALITY (previous best 62.3%) and 55.7 F1 on QASPER.

## Concrete detail
The clustering is soft: Gaussian Mixture Models over UMAP-reduced embeddings, so one chunk can feed several summaries, with the number of clusters picked by BIC. For querying they compare layer-by-layer tree traversal with a "collapsed tree" that puts every node, from any level, into one pool and takes the top matches up to a token budget. Collapsed tree with a 2000-token budget did best on their QASPER subset, so that's what they use.

## What I'd try
Build the tree on a handful of long PDFs and log which level each retrieved node comes from, broken down by question type. My guess is that "summarize/compare" questions pull summaries and factoid questions still pull leaves. I'd also check how much a summary hallucination near the root hurts.
