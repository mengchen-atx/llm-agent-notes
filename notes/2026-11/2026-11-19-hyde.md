# HyDE: retrieve with a fake answer, not the question

**Paper:** Gao, Ma, Lin & Callan, "Precise Zero-Shot Dense Retrieval without Relevance Labels" (ACL 2023, [arXiv:2212.10496](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10496)) · code: [texttron/hyde](https://github.com/texttron/hyde)

## Idea
Queries and documents look different, so embedding a short question and searching for long passages is a mismatch. HyDE (Hypothetical Document Embeddings) sidesteps it: prompt an instruction-following LLM to *write* a passage that would answer the query, embed that passage with an unsupervised encoder (Contriever), and retrieve the real documents nearest to it. The generated passage can be wrong in its details; the encoder's dense bottleneck keeps the "shape" of relevance and the nearest-neighbor search grounds it in the actual corpus.

## Why it matters
No relevance labels and no fine-tuning, yet it lands close to supervised retrievers. On TREC DL19 it reaches 61.3 nDCG@10, versus 44.5 for plain Contriever and 50.6 for BM25 (fine-tuned Contriever gets 62.1). It's also trivial to bolt onto an existing RAG stack: it only changes how the query vector is built.

## Concrete detail
They don't trust a single sample. They draw N hypothetical documents (InstructGPT, temperature 0.7) and average their embeddings, and they also count the original query as one more hypothesis: v = (Σ f(d̂ₖ) + f(q)) / (N+1). That averaging is what keeps one hallucinated passage from dragging the search off course.

## What I'd try
Compare HyDE against query-only embeddings on a domain-specific corpus with a small open model as the generator, and check how latency and recall change as N goes from 1 to 8. I'd also check whether it hurts on keyword-style queries, where BM25 is already strong.
