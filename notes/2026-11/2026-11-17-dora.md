# DoRA: splitting a LoRA update into magnitude and direction

**Paper:** Liu et al., 2024, "DoRA: Weight-Decomposed Low-Rank Adaptation" ([arXiv:2402.09353](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09353)), code at [NVlabs/DoRA](https://github.com/NVlabs/DoRA)

## Idea
DoRA rewrites each pretrained weight as a magnitude vector (the column-wise norm) times a unit-norm direction matrix. The magnitude is trained directly as a small vector. The direction is updated with an ordinary LoRA pair, then renormalized. The authors arrived at this by comparing how full fine-tuning and LoRA change magnitude versus direction. Plain LoRA tends to move both together, while full fine-tuning can change one without much change in the other.

## Why it matters
It is nearly a drop-in replacement for LoRA. The paper reports that it beats LoRA when fine-tuning LLaMA, LLaVA, and VL-BART, and that after merging there is no extra inference cost. The gain is said to be largest at low ranks, which is where I usually want to be on a single GPU.

## Concrete detail
In PEFT it is a single flag: `LoraConfig(use_dora=True, ...)`. The docs list some limits. Only linear and Conv2D layers are supported, and training is slower than plain LoRA because the weight norm is recomputed on every forward pass. They also recommend merging the weights before inference.

## What I'd try
Run LoRA and DoRA at r=4 and r=16 on the same instruction set. Track step time as well as eval loss, to see whether the low-rank gain holds up once you account for the extra compute.
