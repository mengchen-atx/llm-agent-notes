# QLoRA: the double-quantization arithmetic

**Paper:** Dettmers, Pagnoni, Holtzman, Zettlemoyer, 2023, "QLoRA: Efficient Finetuning of Quantized LLMs" ([arXiv:2305.14314](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14314))

## Idea
Freeze the base model in 4-bit, keep LoRA adapters in 16-bit, and backpropagate through the quantized weights into the adapters. Three pieces make it fit: the NF4 data type (quantile-based, built for normally distributed weights), Double Quantization of the per-block scaling constants, and paged optimizers that use NVIDIA unified memory to absorb spikes from long sequences. The headline: a 65B model fine-tuned on a single 48GB GPU, versus more than 780 GB for regular 16-bit fine-tuning.

## Why it matters
It's the reason "fine-tune a 7B–13B model on one consumer GPU" is a normal weekend project now. The same paper also has an easy-to-miss finding: LoRA on just the query/value projections did not match full fine-tuning; adapters on all linear layers did, and rank mattered much less than that.

## Concrete detail
Block-wise 4-bit quantization with a block size of 64 and an FP32 scale per block costs 32/64 = 0.5 extra bits per parameter. Double Quantization stores those scales in FP8 with their own FP32 scale per 256 blocks: 8/64 + 32/(64·256) ≈ 0.127 bits, saving about 0.373 bits per parameter, roughly 3 GB on a 65B model. Their default recipe was NF4 + DQ, bf16 compute, r=64, alpha=16, LoRA on every linear layer.

## What I'd try
In `transformers`, `BitsAndBytesConfig(load_in_4bit=True, bnb_4bit_quant_type="nf4", bnb_4bit_use_double_quant=True)`, plus `target_modules="all-linear"` in PEFT. Then compare peak memory and eval loss against q/v-only targeting at the same rank on a small instruction set.
