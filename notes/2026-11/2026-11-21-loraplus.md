# LoRA+: give B a bigger learning rate than A

**Paper:** Hayou, Ghosh & Yu, "LoRA+: Efficient Low Rank Adaptation of Large Models" ([arXiv:2402.12354](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12354)) · code: [nikhil-ghosh-berkeley/loraplus](https://github.com/nikhil-ghosh-berkeley/loraplus) · in PEFT: `peft.optimizers.create_loraplus_optimizer`

## Idea
Vanilla LoRA trains the two adapter matrices A and B with the same learning rate. The paper uses a large-width scaling argument to show this is suboptimal: as the embedding dimension grows, a single shared learning rate can't make both A and B learn features efficiently. The fix is tiny: keep the optimizer, but set η_B = λ · η_A with a fixed ratio λ > 1.

## Why it matters
It costs nothing extra at train time and needs no architecture change. The authors report 1-2% better final performance and up to ~2x faster fine-tuning at the same compute as LoRA.

## Concrete detail
The "right" λ isn't universal. With their Init[2] setup on RoBERTa, λ ≈ 2^4 worked well; with Init[1] the best ratio was smaller (around 2^2-2^3), and for their Llama experiments it was around 2^1-2^2. In PEFT you pass `loraplus_lr_ratio` alongside `lr` (which becomes η_A), and the docstring suggests lowering the base lr a bit (e.g. by 2x) compared to a vanilla LoRA run.

## What I'd try
A small sweep over λ ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8, 16} on one instruction-tuning set with a 7B model, keeping η_A fixed, and plotting loss-vs-step to see whether the speedup shows up early or only at the end. I'd also check whether it stacks with DoRA or just overlaps with what the magnitude vector already does.
