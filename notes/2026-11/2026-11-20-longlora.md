# LongLoRA: long-context fine-tuning with shifted group attention

**Paper:** Chen, Qian, Tang, Lai, Liu, Han & Jia, "LongLoRA: Efficient Fine-tuning of Long-Context Large Language Models" ([arXiv:2309.12307](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.12307)) · code: [dvlab-research/LongLoRA](https://github.com/dvlab-research/LongLoRA)

## Idea
Extending a model's context window by fine-tuning is expensive mostly because of attention: the paper points out that training at 8192 tokens costs 16× the self-attention compute of 2048. LongLoRA makes two cuts. During *training* it swaps dense attention for **shifted sparse attention (S²-Attn)**: the sequence is split into groups and attention runs inside each group. At *inference* the model keeps ordinary full attention, so the architecture doesn't change.

## Why it matters
Plain LoRA turns out to be weak at context extension. The fix is surprisingly cheap: also make the **embedding and normalization layers trainable**. In Llama2-7B the norm layers are ≤0.004% of the parameters. With both changes they extend Llama2-7B from 4k to 100k context, or Llama2-70B to 32k, on a single 8×A100 machine.

## Concrete detail
Group-local attention alone would never let information cross group boundaries. So in half of the attention heads the tokens are shifted by half a group before grouping, which gives neighbouring groups an overlap, much like Swin Transformer's shifted windows. For example, an 8192-token run uses group size 2048. The authors say it's about two lines of code in training. Position handling follows Position Interpolation.

## What I'd try
In PEFT, add `modules_to_save=["embed_tokens", "norm"]` and compare it against a LoRA-only baseline when stretching a small Llama to 4× its native context, using passkey retrieval as the check.
