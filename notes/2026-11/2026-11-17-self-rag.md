# Self-RAG: let the model decide when to retrieve

**Paper:** Asai et al., 2023, "Self-RAG: Learning to Retrieve, Generate, and Critique through Self-Reflection" ([arXiv:2310.11511](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11511)), project page at [selfrag.github.io](https://selfrag.github.io/)

## Idea
Standard RAG always fetches a fixed number of passages, whether the question needs them or not. Self-RAG trains a single LM to emit special *reflection tokens*. These tokens decide whether to retrieve at all, grade whether each passage is relevant, check whether the output is supported by the passage, and rate how useful the answer is overall.

## Why it matters
The model's own critique becomes something you can control at inference time. You can push it toward stricter citations or toward more fluent answers without retraining.

## Concrete detail
The training cost is lower than I expected. The reflection tokens are inserted into the training corpus offline by a separate critic model, which was trained on labels collected from GPT-4, so no critic has to be running while the generator trains. At inference, retrieval can be triggered with a threshold on the probability of the "retrieve = yes" token.

## What I'd try
Sweep that retrieval threshold on a mixed set of factual and chit-chat questions, and plot accuracy against the number of retrieval calls.
